§03Work

Three problems, and how I'd build themWorked examples

Worked examples, not client records — the shape of problem I take on, and the approach I'd bring.

WE-01 · Multi-tenant field-service platform
Logistics · 6–8 wk
Technicians across several franchises run on paper job cards; head office reconciles by hand and sees a closed job days late. Each franchise's data must stay separate.
Approach Spring Boot modular monolith — not microservices at this size — with tenant isolation enforced at row level in Postgres rather than in application code, so a missed filter cannot leak data. Angular PWA queues work offline, reconciles on reconnect.
WE-02 · Subscription billing and payments
B2B SaaS · 5–7 wk
Subscriptions tracked in spreadsheets. Failed cards go unnoticed, upgrades are pro-rated by hand, and finance cannot reconcile revenue against the provider.
Approach The hard part is not the Stripe call, it is the same webhook arriving twice. A double-entry ledger keyed on the provider event ID makes replays inert by construction; then dunning, proration, and a console finance runs without an engineering ticket.
WE-03 · Legacy rescue: Struts/JSP → Spring Boot
Manufacturing · phased
A decade-old internal app, no tests, nobody left who wrote it. Reports take tens of seconds and releases are quarterly because each one is frightening.
Approach Not a rewrite — those fail. Strangler-fig behind a gateway: new Spring Boot modules take traffic a screen at a time while the old app keeps serving, so there is no cutover weekend. Characterisation tests first, then index and N+1 work.
