§03Work

Three problems, and how I'd build them

Worked examples rather than client records — the shape of problem I take on, and the approach I'd bring to each. Written this way on purpose: I'd rather show you the thinking than pad the page with logos you can't verify. Measured records replace these as projects complete.

WE-01

Multi-tenant field service platform

Worked example Logistics
The situation

Technicians across several franchises run on paper job cards. Head office reconciles them by hand and only sees a completed job days later. Every franchise wants its own data kept separate.

How I'd build it

A Spring Boot modular monolith — not microservices at this size — with row-level tenant isolation enforced in Postgres rather than in application code, so a missed filter can't leak another franchise's jobs. An Angular PWA that queues work offline and reconciles on reconnect, photo evidence straight to S3 with presigned URLs, and a supervisor approval step before a job closes.

WE-02

Billing and payments for a B2B SaaS

Worked example SaaS
The situation

Subscriptions tracked in spreadsheets. Failed cards go unnoticed for weeks, upgrades are pro-rated by hand, and finance can't reconcile the revenue number against the payment provider.

How I'd build it

The hard part isn't the Stripe call, it's what happens when the same webhook arrives twice. A double-entry ledger keyed on the provider's event ID, so replays are inert by construction rather than by a de-dup table someone forgets to check. Then automatic dunning and retries, proration on plan change, and an internal console finance can operate without filing an engineering ticket.

WE-03

Legacy rescue: Struts/JSP to Spring Boot + Angular

Worked example Manufacturing
The situation

A decade-old internal app with no tests and nobody left who wrote it. Reports take tens of seconds, releases happen quarterly because each one is frightening, and every change risks breaking something unrelated.

How I'd build it

Not a rewrite — those fail. A strangler-fig migration behind a gateway: new Spring Boot modules take traffic one screen at a time while the old app keeps serving everything else, so there's no cutover weekend and no freeze. Characterisation tests first to pin current behaviour, then query and index work on the reports, then a pipeline that deploys on merge so releases stop being events.

§04Process

What the six weeks actually look like

Timings below are for a typical fixed-scope build. Nothing here is a surprise once we start — you always know what week you're in and what lands next.

W0

Scoping call and written quote

Thirty to sixty minutes on the problem, users and constraints. You get back a scope document, an architecture sketch and one fixed number. If the fit is wrong, I say so and suggest who is better placed.

W1

Foundations

Repository, data model, migrations, auth, environments and the deployment pipeline — before feature work, not after. By Friday there is a real URL you can open, even if it only logs in.

W2–W5

Build, demo, adjust

Features ship to staging continuously. Every Friday: a short recorded walkthrough, what moved, what's next, and anything that threatens the date. Small course corrections are free — a change in scope becomes a written change order.

W6

Hardening and go-live

Load-sensitive queries, error handling, backups, monitoring and alerts. Production deploy with a rollback path, plus a walkthrough session for whoever operates it day to day.

POST

Thirty-day warranty

Anything that doesn't behave as specified gets fixed at no cost. New ideas are quoted separately — that boundary is written down up front so neither of us has to argue about it later.

§06Terms

The questions that decide it

Who owns the code?
You do. Full IP assignment transfers on final payment, in writing, in the contract. I keep no licence over your product and I don't reuse client code elsewhere. Generic utilities I wrote before your project stay mine, and you get a perpetual right to use them.
Can you deploy it, or do we handle that?
Either. Deployment is included in fixed-scope builds by default: Docker images, a CI/CD pipeline, environments, TLS, backups and alerts on your AWS account or a VPS. If you have a platform team with their own standards, I build to their conventions and hand over instead — the price drops accordingly.
What happens when the scope changes?
It always changes a little, and small adjustments inside the agreed shape are free. Anything that adds a new screen, entity or integration becomes a one-paragraph change order with its own price and date impact. You approve it before I write the code, so there are never surprise line items on an invoice.
How do payments work?
Fixed-scope: 40% to start, 30% at the mid-point demo, 30% on delivery. Retainers are invoiced monthly in advance. Hourly work is billed every two weeks with a time log attached. Bank transfer, Wise or Payoneer; taxes are added where they apply.
Will you work inside our existing team?
Yes — that's what the retainer is for. Your repo, your branching model, your Jira, your code review. I'll join standups in your working hours and I don't need managing, but I do ask for one person who can answer product questions within a day.
What are your hours and how fast do you reply?
Core overlap of at least four hours with your timezone, agreed before we start. Messages get an answer the same working day; production emergencies get an answer within two hours during the warranty period or an active retainer.
Do you sign NDAs?
Routinely, before any detail is shared. Send yours or use mine. Client names stay off this site unless they've explicitly agreed to be named.
What do you not take on?
Equity-only work, projects with no defined decision-maker, "just make it look like this app" with no scope, and rescue jobs where the previous developer still has production access. I'll also decline anything I can't build to a standard I'd put my name on.
